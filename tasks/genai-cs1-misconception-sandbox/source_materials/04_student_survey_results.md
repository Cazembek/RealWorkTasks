# Student Survey Results: AI Tool Use in CS1

**Administered to:** 132 students across the last two CS1 offerings (voluntary,
anonymous, IRB-exempt as routine course-improvement survey)
**Format:** 5-point Likert items + open-response comments

## Selected quantitative results

| Item | Result |
|---|---|
| "I use a generative AI tool (ChatGPT, Copilot, etc.) at least weekly for this course's assignments." | 71% agree/strongly agree |
| "When AI gives me a fix, I usually understand *why* it works." | 26% agree/strongly agree; 54% disagree/strongly disagree |
| "I'd like a place to practice getting unstuck on bugs that doesn't count toward my grade." | 60% agree/strongly agree |
| "I worry about being flagged for an academic-integrity violation if I use AI help, even when it's allowed." | 45% agree/strongly agree |
| "I only have reliable internet access on campus (lab computer or campus wifi), not at home." | 18% agree/strongly agree |
| "I'd be motivated by earning some kind of badge/achievement for practicing debugging, even if it's not graded." | 39% agree/strongly agree; 31% neutral; 30% disagree |

## Selected open-response comments (anonymized, lightly edited for length)

> "Half the time I don't even read the explanation, I just want it to work and move on.
> I know that's bad but I'm tired."

> "I'd actually use something like this if I knew for sure my professor couldn't see
> that I was 'cheating' by using it — right now I just don't ask for AI help at all near
> deadlines because I'm scared it'll look bad."

> "Please don't make it another leaderboard thing, some of us just want to fix our code
> without a whole gamification layer on top."

> "I use my phone hotspot for most of my coursework outside of lab hours, so anything
> heavy or flashy is annoying to load."

**CTI note:** the tension between "wants low-stakes practice" (60% agree) and "wary of
heavy gamification" (comments) suggests engagement mechanics should be optional/light,
not the core motivator — and that a majority of the felt risk is about *whether the
instructor can see it*, not about capability alone.
